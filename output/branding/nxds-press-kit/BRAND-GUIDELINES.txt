APPROVED POSITIONING
Audience: people building products who need a consistent design system they can make their own. Speak most directly to frontend developers, design engineers, and product teams maintaining a growing interface; remain welcoming to individual builders.
Problem: keeping interfaces consistent as products gain screens, themes, states, frameworks, and contributors.
Core promise: a design system you can own and shape, with consistency built in.

Positioning statement:
Nexus Design System is an open-source foundation for building consistent interfaces that feel like your product. Shared tokens coordinate the design, customizable components give you control, and executable stories make behavior inspectable.

Reasons to choose Nexus:
Consistency — one foundation for design decisions, carried through shared tokens.
Control — customizable appearance and open-source implementation that teams can inspect and adapt.
Confidence — component states and tested behavior can be explored alongside executable examples.

Feature-to-benefit translation:
Shared tokens: make design decisions once and carry them across the interface.
Framework-independent foundation: retain a design language when implementation technologies differ.
Open source: inspect, adapt, and own the implementation.
Customization: express the product's identity rather than inherit a library's default look.
Executable stories: inspect states and exercised behavior alongside examples.

Communication direction:
Lead with the outcome; use framework support, appearance demonstrations and Storybook examples as evidence.
Proposed headline, not yet a locked tagline: Your design language. Every component.
Proposed supporting copy: Build consistent interfaces with shared tokens, customizable components, and open-source code you own.

Claim boundaries:
Distinguish the framework-independent foundation from currently available component bindings. Do not promise support for all frameworks based on planned bindings.
Describe tested stories and behaviors according to actual coverage. Do not imply that executable stories guarantee every state or behavior is tested.
This approval establishes positioning, not an independent audit of current feature or test coverage.
